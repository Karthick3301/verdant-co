Plant Clinic: Frequently Asked Questions

Why arenâ€™t my new leaves showing split fenestrations yet?

expand_more
Fenestrations are a biological adaptation to allow canopy sunlight and heavy tropical rainfall through to lower foliage. If young leaves emerge solid, it is almost always a sign that your Monstera requires more indirect sunlight or has not yet reached botanical maturity (typically at 6â€“10 months).

What should I do with the long brown aerial roots?

expand_more
Do not snip them off unless necessary! In nature, aerial roots climb tree bark and pull ambient moisture from the jungle air. You can gently tuck them back down into the pot substrate to supply supplemental moisture to the plant, or guide them around a moss pole.

How frequently should I dust or clean the foliage?

expand_more
We recommend gently wiping the leaves with a damp microfiber cloth once a month. Removing indoor household dust allows the stomata to transpire properly and improves photosynthetic efficiency by up to 30%.
